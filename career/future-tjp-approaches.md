## The core strategic read

You are a **product manager who became a builder**, sitting inside an agency whose
**clients are the AI vendors themselves**.

That combination is rare and it is the whole game. Most people starting an AI consultancy
spend three years fighting for access to the Microsoft / AWS / Google / Salesforce orbit.
You are already in the room, holding the pen on their brand experiences.

**That access — not your engineering — is your scarcest asset, and its shelf life is tied
to your employment.** Every approach below is a different mechanism for converting it into
something you own before it expires.

Three assets, ranked by scarcity:

1. **Access** to senior decision-makers at major tech brands (scarce, perishable, borrowed)
2. **Hybrid judgment** — product thinking plus hands-on agent building (scarce, durable, yours)
3. **Audience** — newsletter, community, mentoring (currently modest, compounds slowly, fully yours)

The approaches differ mainly in which asset they bet on.

# Approach 1 — The Category Owner

**"AI for live experience"**

### Thesis
Go absurdly narrow and globally deep. Own agentic AI for events, experiential and brand
experience as a *named category that did not exist before you named it*.

### The 10x mechanism
In a niche this specific there are perhaps a dozen credible people worldwide. Being the
obvious #1 in a small category beats being #400 in "AI consulting." The consulting arm sells
to agencies, venue groups, and event-tech vendors who all share the same problems and have
no vocabulary for them yet. You supply the vocabulary.

### Skills and areas to explore
- Real-time multimodal agents (voice, vision, live translation)
- On-site constraints: edge inference, offline-capable, unreliable-network design
- Event data infrastructure: registration, badge scan, CRM, attribution, post-show ROI
- Attendee-experience personalisation at scale
- Live-ops reliability — systems that cannot fail in front of 15,000 people
- The industry's own commercial language: sponsorship economics, delegate acquisition, show ROI

### Horizons

| When | Target |
|---|---|
| **2027** | Publish the definitive *State of AI in Brand Experience* report. Ship 3 named production systems at Nteractive. First trade-press byline. |
| **2031** | Boutique firm, 6–10 people, retained by 15–20 agencies and brands. Mainstage at IMEX / EventTech / Cvent Connect. |
| **2036** | The category's standard-setter. Plausible acquisition by an event-tech platform or agency holding group. |
| **2046** | The person the industry's history credits with the shift. |

### For the organisation
Nteractive becomes the agency with a **defensible AI capability competitors cannot answer** —
a pitch differentiator that wins accounts, not merely an internal efficiency story.

### Personal brand / thought leadership
Trade-press dominance rather than general AI commentary. Narrow audience, enormous share of it.
Annual industry report as the flagship artefact. Conference circuit is the distribution channel.

### Risks
- The events industry is cyclical and slow to buy; a downturn hits your only market.
- If the category stays small, you have built a moat around a pond.
- Category-creation only pays if the category actually forms.

# Approach 2 — The Spinout

**Build the firm inside, then own it**

### Thesis
Do not leave to start a consultancy. Turn Nteractive's AI function into a real service line
with its own P&L, then negotiate to own it — as a spinout, a joint venture, or an equity stake.

### The 10x mechanism
You build a business using someone else's balance sheet, brand, insurance, legal function and
client list. Your first £1m of revenue arrives with a warm client base instead of cold
outreach. Founders spend years buying what you would be handed.

### Skills and areas to explore
Mostly commercial, not technical:
- Pricing and packaging professional services
- SOW and MSA structure; scope control
- Margin management and utilisation
- Hiring and managing a delivery team
- **How to make the equity ask** — the single highest-leverage skill on this path
- Agency M&A basics, so you understand what your service line is actually worth to them

### Horizons

| When | Target |
|---|---|
| **2027** | Move AI from cost centre to billable line item. Land the first three external-facing engagements with your name on the SOW. Open the ownership conversation. |
| **2031** | Run a £2–5m service line, holding equity or a signed spinout agreement. |
| **2036** | Independent firm, 30–60 people — or exited the spinout and onto the second thing. |
| **2046** | Own or chair a group. Allocating capital, not delivering work. |

### For the organisation
You hand them a new revenue stream and a valuation story. **This is the approach where your
ambition and theirs are aligned rather than in quiet conflict** — which is worth more than it
sounds, because it buys you years of runway you would otherwise spend hiding your intentions.

### Personal brand / thought leadership
Deliberately co-branded early. Their logo lends you credibility; borrow it on purpose. Shift
to your own brand only once the commercial structure is signed.

### Risks
- The entire thesis rests on one conversation that may go badly.
- They may want the asset without you owning it.
- **Mitigation: test the appetite early and cheaply, before you have built something too
  valuable for them to let go of.**

# Approach 3 — The Leverage Play

**Product, not hours**

### Thesis
The WhatsApp agent was the signal. Services are the funding mechanism; software is the
destination. Build IP that earns while you sleep, and let consulting exist only to discover
and distribute it.

### The 10x mechanism
Consulting income is linear in your hours and hard-capped by them. Product income is
decoupled. The consulting arm becomes R&D lab and sales channel for the thing you own.

### Skills and areas to explore
The largest skill jump of the five:
- Full product engineering, not prototypes
- Eval harnesses, observability, reliability engineering for LLM systems
- Multi-tenancy, security, SOC2 / ISO 27001
- Self-serve pricing, packaging, onboarding
- Distribution and growth loops
- Fundraising, if that route is chosen

Your PM background is a genuine unfair advantage here — most AI engineers do not have it.

### Horizons

| When | Target |
|---|---|
| **2027** | One paid product with 20+ paying customers, however small. Consulting deliberately capped to fund it. |
| **2031** | **Product revenue exceeds services revenue** — the crossover that defines this path. |
| **2036** | A real software company, or an acquisition behind you. |
| **2046** | Investor / operator. Backing other builders. |

### For the organisation
Nteractive becomes design partner and first customer. **Negotiate the IP position in writing,
now** — before building anything on their time or infrastructure. This is the only approach
with a live legal question at its centre.

### Personal brand / thought leadership
Build in public. Ship, demo, teach. Audience is builders and buyers, not the events trade.
Changelog and demo cadence over essays.

### Risks
- Highest variance of the five.
- The failure mode is specific: "consulting arm" quietly becomes a distraction from a product
  that is not working, and you end up doing both badly.
- Employment IP clauses bite hardest here. Resolve before building.

# Approach 4 — The Trusted Advisor

**Move up, not out**

### Thesis
Stop competing on building; compete on judgment. Become the person CMOs and CTOs at major
tech brands call *before* committing £5m to an AI programme. Small firm, few clients,
very high fees.

### The 10x mechanism
Your rate stops tracking your skill and starts tracking **the size of the decision you are
influencing**. Work is fractional Chief AI Officer engagements, AI strategy and governance
reviews, board education, keynotes. Your existing access to senior people at major tech brands
is precisely the door most advisors cannot open.

### Skills and areas to explore
- Business-case and ROI modelling for AI programmes
- AI governance and risk: EU AI Act, ISO 42001, procurement and vendor diligence
- Organisational change management and AI adoption
- **Executive communication and stage craft** — non-negotiable
- Paid speaking as a business line, not vanity

### Horizons

| When | Target |
|---|---|
| **2027** | Three paid speaking slots. One published framework people cite by name. First advisory retainer alongside the day job. |
| **2031** | Boutique advisory, 3–6 senior people. £400k–£1m+ personal earnings. Book deal or definitive framework in market. |
| **2036** | Board seats. A recognised name in enterprise AI strategy. |
| **2046** | Chair and NED portfolio. Institute or fellowship. |

### For the organisation
You become their most senior AI voice in front of clients — wheeled into pitches, raising the
whole agency's altitude and average deal size.

### Personal brand / thought leadership
The heaviest brand investment of the five. *Decoded by TJP* becomes the flagship: fewer,
longer, sharper pieces aimed at executives rather than practitioners. One signature framework,
relentlessly repeated.

### Risks
- Advisory without recent hands-on depth decays fast — within about two model generations.
- The market is crowded with people who talk well and have shipped nothing.
- **Your edge is that you actually build. Protect it; do not abandon it for the stage.**

# Approach 5 — The Institution Builder

**Community first, consulting as a layer**

### Thesis
Lead with Tech4Dev, Tech Meetup Birmingham and the Nigerian tech diaspora. Build an audience,
then an institution — training, cohorts, an academy, a talent pipeline. The consulting arm is
one monetisation layer on a much larger platform.

### The 10x mechanism
Community is the highest-leverage distribution that exists. Ten thousand people who trust you
means you never sell again — you announce. It also unlocks revenue consulting cannot touch:
cohort programmes, sponsorship, licensing, placement fees. And it gives you a talent bench you
can staff engagements from at a cost structure no UK-only competitor can match.

### Skills and areas to explore
- Curriculum design and teaching
- Community operations and moderation at scale
- Content production at volume
- Partnership and sponsorship sales
- Nonprofit / hybrid structuring and grant funding, if going the full distance

### Horizons

| When | Target |
|---|---|
| **2027** | One paid cohort delivered end to end. *Decoded* at a serious subscriber count with real engagement, not vanity numbers. |
| **2031** | An academy with hundreds of alumni. Consulting arm staffed largely from them. UK–Nigeria delivery model operating. |
| **2036** | An institution with a name bigger than yours. Policy influence, corporate partners, thousands placed. |
| **2046** | Legacy scale — measured in careers created, not revenue. |

### For the organisation
A genuine early-careers AI talent pipeline into Nteractive, and a non-performative DEI and ESG
story that stands up to scrutiny.

### Personal brand / thought leadership
The widest reach of the five and the most durable. Also the most personal — this one is
downstream of your values, and that will show. Your Medium voice, not your LinkedIn voice.

### Risks
- Slowest to monetise.
- Community work expands to fill all available time.
- The failure mode is being **beloved and underpaid**. Needs a hard commercial spine bolted on
  from day one, not retrofitted at year three.

## Comparison

| | Time to first £ | Ceiling | Risk | Depends most on | Primary asset bet |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Category Owner | Medium | High | Low–Med | Niche depth + trade visibility | Judgment |
| 2. Spinout | Fast | High | Low | One negotiation going well | Access |
| 3. Product | Slow | Highest | High | Engineering + distribution | Judgment |
| 4. Advisor | Medium | High | Medium | Brand + exec access | Access |
| 5. Institution | Slowest | Widest | Medium | Patience + audience | Audience |

### Which compose, which conflict

- **1 + 2 compose almost perfectly** — own the category, then spin out the firm that serves it.
- **4 layers onto either 1 or 2** — advisory is a natural altitude gain from a category position.
- **3 and 5 genuinely demand a choice.** Both consume the exact time the others need. Attempting
  either alongside another approach means doing both at half strength.

### The question underneath the question

"10x my career" hides a real decision: **10x on what axis?**

- **Income** → 2, then 4
- **Autonomy** → 3
- **Reach and influence** → 4
- **Legacy** → 5
- **Optionality** → 1, then 2

The comparison table is only honest once that axis is chosen. Pick the axis first; the
approach follows from it.

### Recommendation

**#2 as the vehicle, #1 as the positioning.**

Rationale: it converts your most perishable asset (access) before it expires, it is the
lowest-risk route to actually *owning* something, and it does not require you to first become
a better engineer or a bigger celebrity. It requires you to become a better negotiator this
year — which is both faster and more learnable than the alternatives.

The 2027 milestone that matters most is not technical. It is getting AI moved from cost centre
to billable line item, with your name on the SOW.
