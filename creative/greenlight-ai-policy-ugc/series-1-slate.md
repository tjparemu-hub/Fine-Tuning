# SERIES 1 — six episodes, six clauses

One episode, one rule, one lane. If a staff member watches all six they have covered every
part of the policy that changes what they do on a Tuesday afternoon.

**Lane system** (burned into the top-left of every episode):
🟢 **GREENLIT** — do this freely · 🟡 **CHECK FIRST** — one question stands between you and go
· 🔴 **HARD STOP** — no exception without written sign-off

## Episode briefs

### Ep 1 — "Two Copilots" · 🟢🔴 · 30s
**Room:** desk corner. **Glasses:** on.
Work vs Web is the single highest-frequency decision in the building and the easiest to get
wrong, because both doors say Copilot. Internal, Confidential, Client-Confidential, Restricted
and personal data are all permitted in **Work** and in Copilot inside the Office apps. **None**
of it goes in **Web**. Three carve-outs survive even in Work: legally privileged material stays
out entirely, a contractual AI ban beats the licence, and external meetings aren't recorded or
transcribed without everyone's consent.
**CTA:** check the toggle before you type · Enterprise licence → `helpdesk@nteractive.com`
→ Full shot list: `ep01-work-vs-web-30s.md`

### Ep 2 — "Check the Contract" · 🟡 · 35s
**Room:** meeting room, lights off. **Glasses:** up on head.
Cold open on her scrolling an MSA on a laptop. Some Nteractive contracts prohibit AI outright;
others restrict what data may be processed. You check **before** you use any AI tool on client
work. Account leads own this: identify the restrictions at kickoff, tell the team, and tell IT.
**Beat that lands:** "The tool being approved doesn't make it allowed. The contract outranks the tool."
**CTA:** unsure → ask your account lead, today, not after you've drafted it.

### Ep 3 — "Shadow AI" · 🔴 · 40s
**Room:** kitchen counter. **Glasses:** on.
The three faces of Shadow AI, counted on fingers: a free or personal-tier account, a browser
extension with AI in it, and an AI feature switched on inside a third-party platform nobody
reviewed. That third one catches good people — the "summarise with AI" button that appeared in
a tool we already pay for is a new AI tool, and a new AI feature needs approval before it's
turned on. Approved tools live in the IT registry (Trelica); approval is joint, Head of IT and
Head of ESG.
**Beat that lands:** "If you didn't find it on the approved list, you found Shadow AI."
**CTA:** not sure if it counts as AI? That question *is* the answer — ask IT.

### Ep 4 — "Nothing Ships Unread" · 🟡 · 35s
**Room:** desk corner, different wall. **Glasses:** up on head.
She reads out a fluent, confident, entirely wrong AI paragraph, then finds the error on camera.
Meaningful human review is mandatory for anything internal or client-facing — and the standard
applies to AI output *someone else* generated that you're now working with. Accuracy, quality
and appropriateness are yours regardless of what produced the first draft.
**Beat that lands:** "It's not wrong-looking. That's the problem."
**CTA:** read it as if you wrote it. Because as far as the client is concerned, you did.

### Ep 5 — "Don't Build It Alone" · 🔴 · 40s
**Room:** stairwell. **Glasses:** on.
Vibe-coding a tool over a weekend is the fastest-growing risk in the business. Four triggers,
any one of which means IT reviews it **before** you build, test or ship: it's reachable from
outside Nteractive; it touches client, supplier or third-party data; it connects to our systems,
APIs, MCPs or credentials; or it's meant for real commercial or client use. Agentic tools raise
the stakes — they act with limited human input, so permissions, monitoring and approval
checkpoints aren't optional.
**Beat that lands:** "Prototype on your own time with fake data, and it's a hobby. Point it at a
client and it's a system — and systems get reviewed."
**CTA:** building something? Tell IT while it's still an idea. Not escalating is itself a breach.

### Ep 6 — "Say It Early" · 🟢 · 35s
**Room:** corridor walk-and-talk. **Glasses:** up on head.
The six reportable situations, said plainly and without menace: unapproved tool on client work;
personal or client-confidential data in a tool not approved for it; AI content sent to a client
unread; AI used on a restricted engagement; a suspected breach involving an AI tool; a tool
behaving as though it reached data it shouldn't have. Then the line that makes the whole series
work: **prompt, good-faith self-reports are not disciplined for the report itself.** And the
honest other half — deliberate, repeated or concealed breaches are treated differently, and a
serious breach can be gross misconduct.
**CTA:** report it the same way as any security incident. Today beats tidy.
